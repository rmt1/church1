story:
- when: Before 1040
  what: A hospice for pilgrims stood on Monte Scalari, near the road the agent identifies as the Via Cassia Adrianea. A monastic community is recorded here from 1040.
- when: 11th and 12th centuries
  what: Gifts from the lords of the nearby castle of Cintoia turned the hospice into an abbey of the Vallombrosan order, a Benedictine reform founded at Vallombrosa, east of Florence.
- when: '1212'
  what: Ranieri, Bishop of Fiesole, consecrated the church. It has a Latin-cross plan and stone altars.
- when: 14th to 17th centuries
  what: 'The monks enlarged the abbey: new monastic rooms, the cellars, an apartment for the abbot and cells for every monk.'
- when: '1505'
  what: A terracotta coat of arms, attributed to the Della Robbia workshop, was set over the main door.
- when: '1776'
  what: With few monks left, the abbey was suppressed.
- when: '1785'
  what: The church became an ordinary parish church.
- when: Second World War
  what: German troops destroyed the bell tower. Its bell chamber had been the one part of the abbey never altered. Only the foundations are left.
- when: '2026'
  what: For sale through Romolini at €2,000,000, with two farmhouses and about 100 hectares.

seen:
- From the air the abbey is a closed block of ranges round two courtyards, roofed in terracotta tile. The roofs are on throughout. Ivy has climbed to the eaves on at least two sides.
- The outer wall along the track is coursed stone, about three storeys high, with small openings and a single arched door.
- The first cloister is paved in stone slabs with a carved doorway and oil jars. The walls round it are stained and weeds grow from the upper ledges.
- The loggia above has stone columns and a tile and timber roof in fair order. Its floor is stacked with salvaged tiles and timber, and part of its parapet is loose dry stone.
- The cellar is brick cross vaults on square piers, dry, with a stone floor.
- 'Several rooms are furnished: a sitting room with a stone hooded fireplace, a study with books and a radiator, a bedroom, a working kitchen. Somebody lives or lived in this wing.'
- Other rooms are bare stone with tile and beam ceilings. One vaulted passage has cracked and stained plaster, and a wash-house has lost most of its plaster.
- One farmhouse is a roofless shell under ivy with a solar panel on the ground beside it. The other has its roof.

The purchase is the cheap part. Whoever takes this on needs ten million more and ten years. I would do it as a house with a church, keep the monks' plan, and rebuild the bell tower the Germans blew up.
