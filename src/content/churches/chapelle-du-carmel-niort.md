story:
- when: '1648'
  what: Louis XIV and Anne of Austria sign letters patent for a Carmel at Niort on 16 July. The first nuns come mostly from Paris and settle in the former Hôtel de Barbezières on the edge of town.
- when: '1675'
  what: They move into a monastery built for them by the architect François Le Duc, called Toscane, near the Porte Mellaise.
- when: October 1792
  what: The Revolution expels the 26 nuns and takes the monastery. They live in hiding in small groups. The community dies out in 1808.
- when: '1858'
  what: Six nuns from the Carmel of Poitiers come back to Niort and lodge in a gardener's house at Clos-Martin, on the heights above the town.
- when: '1859'
  what: The prioress, Sister Emmanuel de Sainte Sophie, starts a new monastery on the rue de Strasbourg. She leads the community for about forty years, and the building takes twenty.
- when: November 1868
  what: The monastery church is consecrated by Monseigneur Pie, bishop of Poitiers. This is the chapel now for sale.
- when: '1879'
  what: The last wing of the cloister is begun.
- when: 1901 to 1919
  what: The laws against religious congregations drive the nuns into exile at Thumaide in Belgium for eighteen years. They return to the rue de Strasbourg in 1919.
- when: 1933 to 1955
  what: Two sisters leave for the Carmels of Chongqing and Kunming in China. In 1940 the house shelters a Belgian community fleeing the invasion, and in 1955 it takes in sixteen Carmelites from Hanoi for two years.
- when: '2005'
  what: The interior of the chapel is cleaned.
- when: '2006'
  what: The monastery has become too hard to keep up. The community decides to build a smaller one at Bessines, outside the town.
- when: October 2009
  what: The nuns move to Bessines. The chapel is deconsecrated the same year.
- when: '2010'
  what: The convent is sold. Its site is later marketed for housing.
- when: '2021'
  what: The Bessines community, down to nine sisters, closes. The Carmel of Niort ends, 373 years after its founding.
- when: November 2023
  what: The chapel is put up for sale at 250,000 euros through Patrice Besse, on behalf of a La Rochelle property company.
- when: April 2025
  what: It is relisted at 220,000 euros. The local paper notes it has stood empty for fifteen years.
- when: '2026'
  what: Espaces Atypiques lists it at 190,000 euros.

Carmelite nuns prayed here for 140 years and left in 2009. The stone interior is in good order and every window still has its glass. The price is €190,000, down from €250,000 in 2023.

It would suit concerts: open the doors, dry it out and put music in it. A roof inspection is the first step.
