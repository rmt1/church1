story:
- when: '1621'
  what: The convent is founded inside the walls of Saint-Amour. The first nuns arrive from Pontarlier the following year. The town already has Augustinians (1438) and Capuchins (1620), and the Visitandines follow in 1633.
- when: 18th century
  what: A large building campaign gives the convent its cloister and the two parallel ranges with arcaded galleries that survive today.
- when: '1790'
  what: The Revolution suppresses all four convents in the town. They are sold as national property.
- when: 19th and 20th centuries
  what: The buildings serve as a school, with classrooms on the first floors and dormitories above. At some point the church and the south wing of the cloister are pulled down. Part of the choir is left standing.
- when: 1960s
  what: A prefabricated parish hall goes up in the former orchard.
- when: 7 June 2013
  what: The whole monastery is inscrit as a monument historique, including the ground of the cloister court and the sites of the demolished church, chapel and cloister wings. The State record notes that the orchard may hide remains of the chapel and wells.
- when: Today
  what: The State record lists three of the five plots as communal and two as private. The convent's papers are in the Jura departmental archives, series 42 H.

A convent founded in 1621 for the price of a studio flat, and every square metre of it needs work. The chapel was mostly pulled down. Its choir survives, 104 square metres under rib vaults, between two wings with seven-arch arcades onto the cloister court.

This is the twenty-year vocation on the list. The roof frame is sound, and that is where the good news stops. Take it on with a trust, an architect and a plan for the thousand square metres that are not chapel.
