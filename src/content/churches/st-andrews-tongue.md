story:
- when: Before 1680
  what: A chapel of St Peter, Teampull Pheader, stood on the site. The listing record says the core of the present church is probably that medieval chapel.
- when: '1680'
  what: Donald Mackay, Master of Reay, rebuilt the church. The date is on the building. The Mackays of Reay were the chiefs of the clan and their burial vault lies under the gallery.
- when: 1729 to 1731
  what: First round of additions.
- when: '1778'
  what: Further additions. The panelled front of the laird's loft is 18th-century work.
- when: '1862'
  what: Last enlargement. The bellcote is 19th-century.
- when: '1951'
  what: The carved canopy of the Reay loft went to the National Museum of Antiquities in Edinburgh, where it has stayed.
- when: '1971'
  what: Listed Category A.
- when: '2025'
  what: The Church of Scotland declared the building surplus. On 2 August a public meeting in Tongue voted to look at a community purchase. In October a conservation architect surveyed it.
- when: '2026'
  what: 'UpNorth Development Trust published the plan on behalf of the village: buy for £20,000, spend £50,000 in the first year, about £300,000 in all. The steering group hoped for charity registration in July.'

The asking price is offers over £20,000. A conservation architect has surveyed the church and a quantity surveyor has priced the repairs at about £220,000, so a buyer knows what the work is before starting.

The village has formed a group to buy the church and has a plan for it. A buyer who wants this church to stand might work with the group or support it.
