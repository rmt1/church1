story:
- when: Before 1480
  what: A chapel of St Michael stood on the hill. Norman stonework, including a corbel, turned up in the south chapel during the rebuilding of 1912.
- when: About 1480
  what: Peter Courtenay, Bishop of Exeter, paid for a new church. The Courtenays were Earls of Devon.
- when: Early 16th century
  what: John Takell, a merchant, and his wife Joan paid to rebuild the chancel and add the aisles and the north porch. Joan died in 1529 and is buried in the north chapel.
- when: '1617'
  what: Thomas Marwood, physician to Elizabeth I, was buried under a black marble tomb.
- when: '1770'
  what: Date stamped into the lead of the tower roof, which also carries 19th-century outlines of shoes scratched by visitors.
- when: 1835 to 1838
  what: St Paul's was built in the High Street and became the parish church. St Michael's became a chapel of ease.
- when: '1896'
  what: E H Harbottle restored the church and took out the galleries.
- when: '1911'
  what: A fire gutted the interior and left little more than the walls and tower. A window of 1827 at the west end survived.
- when: '1912'
  what: C E Ponting rebuilt it and it was rededicated within the year.
- when: '1935'
  what: Frederick Courtenay, who had been rector and mayor of Honiton, succeeded as Earl of Devon.
- when: '1926'
  what: Herbert Read made the parclose screens.

One hundred thousand pounds for a bishop's church of 1480 with a physician to Elizabeth I buried in it. It burned in 1911 and was back in use in a year. Nobody has managed that kind of speed since 2012.

The Church wants a community use and the Earl of Devon has backed one. If you have the money and no wish to live in it, buy it with a local trust and give Honiton its church back.
