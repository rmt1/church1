story:
- when: '1831'
  what: William Henry Playfair designed the church for Gilbert Elliot, 2nd Earl of Minto, who was laying out a new village. Playfair built the Royal Scottish Academy and the National Gallery in Edinburgh. His only other known church is St Stephen's in Edinburgh.
- when: '1904'
  what: Andrew Carnegie offered £100 towards a pipe organ and the congregation accepted. The 4th Earl of Minto, then Governor-General of Canada, objected that the heritors had not been asked. The row ran in the newspapers all summer. The Earl ended it by paying most of the cost himself, and the Ingram organ went in that September.
- when: '1921'
  what: 'A war memorial by Thomas Clapperton was unveiled in the churchyard: a soldier advancing, life size. The face is said to be that of Lieutenant Esmond Elliot, the Earl''s youngest son, who died of wounds at Ypres in 1917.'
- when: '1926'
  what: Stained glass was added.
- when: '1934'
  what: The interior was refitted with new pews, pulpit, lectern and communion table. The organ was moved and a chancel window by James Wright of Glasgow went in, in memory of the minister who had fought for the organ.
- when: '2007'
  what: Listed Category B.
- when: '2026'
  what: Closed in March and put up for sale at offers over £75,000.

Seventy-five thousand pounds for a church by the man who built the National Gallery of Scotland. It closed eight months ago and it shows: the paint is clean and the roof is straight.

I would buy this one before any of the others. It has water, an organ with a story, and no work that cannot wait for a survey.
