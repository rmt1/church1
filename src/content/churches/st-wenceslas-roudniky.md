story:
- when: '1352'
  what: First written record of the church. The nave is the oldest part standing.
- when: 1375 to 1400
  what: The Gothic chancel and sacristy were built.
- when: About 1480
  what: A Prague workshop painted a pair of altar wings for the church, which was then Utraquist. They were later cut down and hidden under Baroque marbling, which is how they survived.
- when: '1486'
  what: Date given for the rib vault of the chancel.
- when: 1500 to 1525
  what: The tower was finished.
- when: '1607'
  what: Rudolf von Bünau paid for the sandstone altar, carved by Hörnigk of Pirna in Saxony.
- when: '1711'
  what: A Baroque onion dome was put on the tower. The cemetery gate followed in 1713.
- when: 1878 to 1879
  what: General repair to plans by the Prague builder Achilles Wolf. The tower got the roof it has now.
- when: '1914'
  what: Drainage, new plaster and terrazzo floors.
- when: 1960s
  what: Heritage protection was removed so the village could be mined for brown coal. The mine stopped a few dozen metres from the church. A wall painting was found in 1966.
- when: 1970s
  what: The church was repaired. The organ of 1882 was brought from St Martin's in Tuchomyšl, a village the mine destroyed.
- when: '2013'
  what: The parish of Trmice went insolvent and a Prague couple bought the church. They repaired the roofs and the organ and held services.

  what: For sale at 4.49 million crowns.

Four and a half million crowns buys a church that was already old when the Hussites painted its altar. It has a stone altar from 1607, an organ that plays, and a graveyard the town mows for you.

The mine took Tuchomyšl and stopped at the edge of Roudníky. The church is still here because of a few dozen metres. Buy it, keep the services and the concerts going, and get an engineer to look at the cracks before you sign.
