story:
- when: October 1836
  what: The foundation stone was laid. Joseph Feilden of Witton Park gave the land, £200 towards the building and £50 for the glass.
- when: 1836 to 1838
  what: Edmund Sharpe designed it as his first commission. It cost £1,700, with £700 of that from two church building societies. It was consecrated on 10 June 1838.
- when: '1838'
  what: Thomas Willement made the east window.
- when: '1870'
  what: E G Paley, Sharpe's former partner, added the south transept as a mausoleum for the Feildens.
- when: 1881 to 1887
  what: Paley and Austin restored the church and added the five-sided north transept and the vestry.
- when: About 1920
  what: The chancel screen was installed.
- when: '1974'
  what: Listed Grade II*.
- when: '2005'
  what: The church became part of the Parish of Christ the King.

  what: Put up for sale as redundant.

seen:
- The west front is a broad gable with round-headed windows and a small porch. Behind it the octagonal tower rises over the chancel with a gable on each face and a short stone spire.
- The stone is squared sandstone, dark with soot. The courses are tight and the Lombard frieze under the eaves is complete.
- Downpipes run down the nave wall and look intact. Leaves and moss lie along the plinth.
- The slate roofs are even where they show between the trees.
- Inside, the nave has pews, a red carpet and a flat ceiling painted pink between dark beams.
- The west gallery has a panelled front, with glazed doors and a screen below it.
- The walls are painted cream and are clean. There is children's work pinned up along one side.

Edmund Sharpe's first building, Grade II*, with a Willement window, and nobody in Blackburn has wanted it in eight years. That tells you about Blackburn's property market and nothing about the church.

Make an offer well under the old £125,000. It can be a school or a hall tomorrow, and the planners have already said how a house would work.
