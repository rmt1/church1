seen:
- The front has four stone columns under a pediment. The stone of the columns, entablature and pediment is dark green and black with algae. The harled panels between are painted white and are clean by comparison.
- The pediment is faced in slate or stone slabs, stained, with the bell hanging in a round opening.
- The three upper windows are round-headed timber sashes with fanlights, painted white and intact.
- From the air the main roof is slate, even in colour, with a flat central section where the steeple stood.
- The church half has yellow walls, a panelled dado, pews with red cushions and a gallery with a cast-iron balustrade. The roof trusses are open and dark.
- The hall half has a varnished board floor, painted walls, radiators and a suspended light grid. A modern fitted kitchen opens off it.
- No ceiling stains show in either half.

One hundred and ninety-five thousand pounds for a Category A building by Robert Mylne in the middle of a town that tourists drive to on purpose. You get a church and a hall, both in use until recently.

This is a project for someone who wants to run something: concerts, a market, a museum of the town. Budget as much again for the stonework, and talk to the council before you talk to a solicitor.
