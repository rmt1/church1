story:
- when: Early centuries
  what: Libertino, by tradition the first bishop of Agrigento, was stoned and killed on the hill then called the Piano degli Zingari. His body was never found.
- when: '1625'
  what: During a plague the people of the city prayed to him and dug on the hill for his bones. They found nothing. The city officials granted the ground for a chapel and a hospice, on condition that digging for the relics stay allowed.
- when: 17th century
  what: The chapel was enlarged into a church under Bishop Francesco Trahina. A congregation of canons and priests met in it. It had altars to the Ecce Homo and to the Virgin of the Rosary.
- when: '1848'
  what: Bishop Lo Jacono, who restored the church, obtained from Pius IX a proper office for the saint's feast. A later bishop, Lagumina, restored it again.
- when: 1950s
  what: The church became a parish cinema. A new block was built round the main door and its staircase.
- when: '2016'
  what: The Region of Sicily declared the church of cultural interest on 1 February and authorised its sale on 9 June.
- when: '2022'
  what: The church was entered in the FAI's Places of the Heart campaign as a former cine-theatre, privately owned, with a plan to reopen it as one.
- when: '2026'
  what: For sale through Studio Atenea at €32,000, the price reduced.

Thirty-two thousand euros, slightly negotiable, for the church Agrigento built on the spot where its first bishop was stoned. It was a cinema for seventy years and still has the stage.

The Region cleared it for sale in 2016 and nobody has taken it. Fix the roof, clear the floor and show films in it again.
