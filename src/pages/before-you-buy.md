<p class="standfirst">For someone who has never owned a building older than their grandparents. Read it before you call the agent.</p>

## Ten things to do first

1. **Visit with an architect before you offer.** A half-day with a conservation architect and a structural engineer costs a few thousand euros and tells you whether the roof figure is 60,000 or 600,000.
2. **Meet the mayor, the vicar and the neighbours.** In a French village the mairie decides how pleasant the next twenty years will be. Ask what the village hopes will happen to the building.
3. **Water first.** Gutters, downpipes, drains, slipped slates, ventilation. Nine tenths of decay is water, and the first year's budget goes there.
4. **Decide how to hold it.** In your own name, through a company (an SCI in France), or through an association or charitable trust that can receive gifts and outlive you. Take advice in the country concerned.
5. **Know what it costs to own.** Insurance from a specialist, property tax, an annual inspection, security against lead and bell theft.
6. **Ask what is inside.** Get an inventory. Protected altarpieces, statues and bells stay with the building. Organs need playing.
7. **Ask about the graveyard.** Who owns it, who mows it, who may visit, whether burials continue.
8. **Plan a use.** Heritage open days, concerts, a mass on the patronal feast, a choir residency. A church that opens ten days a year has friends.
9. **Plan the exit.** Decide now who gets it after you: a child, the commune, a trust. Put it in the will.
10. **Join a society.** SPAB in Britain and Ireland; Vieilles Maisons Françaises and La Demeure Historique in France. All run courses and answer the phone.

## Heritage status by country

This is a general guide and not legal or tax advice. Rules change; confirm with a notaire, solicitor or accountant in the country concerned.

| France | *Classé* monument historique (national interest); *inscrit* (regional interest); unprotected but within 500 m of a monument | Classé: authorisation from the regional culture office (DRAC) before any restoration, with a qualified heritage architect. Inscrit: building permit with DRAC agreement. ## The three verdicts

Every church on the shortlist carries one of three verdicts.

- **Preserve as found.** The protection covers the interior and the fittings. You are a custodian and nothing else.
- **Light use.** The building must stay one open space, but concerts, exhibitions and occasional worship are welcome.
- **Convertible.** The authorities would accept a new use, within limits. We list few of these.

## A glossary

- **Désaffectation.** The French legal act that removes a church from worship, by prefectoral order with the bishop's consent.
- **Classé, inscrit.** The two grades of French monument historique.
- **DRAC.** The regional office of the French culture ministry. It approves works and awards grants.
- **ABF.** Architecte des Bâtiments de France, the State architect who rules on anything near a protected monument.
- **Listed building consent.** British permission to alter a listed building, inside or out.
- **Faculty.** The Church of England's own permission system, which applies while a church is still in use.
- **Vincolo.** The Italian heritage restriction registered against a building.
- **Soprintendenza.** The regional Italian heritage authority.
