title: Why save them
description: Why old Churches are worth saving, and what heritage custodianship is.
---

For hundreds of years, Churches were the cradle of society. They were how new life, rich or poor, was welcomed into the community. Couples stood before an altar and made a promise, not just to each other, but in front of God. Death was mourned, war was mourned. In their most desperate moments, people fled to their Church and prayed with all their might for a better outcome. Churches are where people have come to seek hope. And the walls of even the lowliest Churches are still full of that energy: the human striving, the hopes and the prayers, the love and the beauty, but also the sadness of life.

The decline of Churches in communities across the West is very real. But I don't believe you need to be Christian to recognise the value in saving these buildings. You just have to respect history. The preservation of our history is enough.

If you go and stand in such Churches intentionally and quieten your mind, you can feel this energy, ever-present, like an atmospheric residue. Sometimes it suffocates and overwhelms, sometimes it enlightens and inspires, but it is always there waiting for us to notice it. And for these reasons we should come into these buildings with respect for all those who stood there before us.

Beauty is receding from daily life, and the buildings that once held entire civilizations together are sold to developers who see square footage and not the centuries. Many of us complain about the death of sacred spaces and of the crafts that built them. Complaining is not enough. We must turn that passion into real action.

I call that action heritage custodianship. You take on a historical building, you repair it, you keep it standing, and you hand it on in better condition than you found it. I think it should be a far greater force in philanthropy than it is.

Another effect of heritage custodianship is that the investment creates demand for the skills that make buildings like these possible. Excellent tradesmen in masonry, carpentry, glass and more are disappearing, but putting them to work on buildings like these builds demand and training for more. If we want more good buildings, we need those skills. We should ask what it means for our descendants to grow up in a world with no examples of things like stained glass windows, and no belief that such things can be made.

Millions of us travel across the world to see ancient sites. Many of those buildings were religious, and they survived because each new faith took them over and kept using them. The Parthenon was a temple, then a Church, then a mosque. The Pantheon in Rome was a temple to the Roman gods and has been a Church since 609. The pyramids are still here only because their sheer mass meant nobody could take them apart. Every generation thinks its own relics are not that important. But centuries from now our descendants will be glad that we preserved our more recent past, so that they too can visit these places and experience what life was like.

This website stemmed from a social media post encouraging more people to think about preserving Churches, after I took one on myself. I have experienced the pushback that comes with taking on a building like this. People will say they are not good financial investments, that they will need continuous repair, and that you will have nobody to sell them to. I contend that perhaps in this you will find the whole point. Not everything is measurable. Each building like this is the start of a beautiful journey that connects you to humanity's past and to our role in the future.
