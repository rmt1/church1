title: Why save them
description: A petition for more philanthropy towards historical buildings, specifically Churches.
---

For hundreds of years, Churches were the cradle of society. They were how new life, rich or poor, was welcomed into the community. Every pair of lovers stood before an altar and made a promise not just to each other, but in front of God. Deaths were mourned, war was mourned. In their most desperate moments, people fled to their Church and prayed with all their might for a better outcome. Churches are where people come and seek hope. And the walls of even the lowliest Churches are still full of that energy to me: the human striving, the hopes and the prayers, the love, beauty, but also sadness of life.

If you go and stand in such Churches intentionally and quieten your mind, you can feel all this energy, ever-present, like an atmospheric residue. Sometimes it suffocates and overwhelms, sometimes it enlightens and inspires, but it is always there waiting for us to notice it. And for these reasons, for every tear shed at the altar, every lover who committed to their partner, for every child's life that was mourned, I come into that building with respect for all those who stood there before me.

Beauty is receding from daily life. The forces of modernity dismiss religion, having never read religious texts, and the buildings that once held entire civilizations together are sold to developers who see square footage where we see centuries. I realize the fallacy in complaining about the death of the arts and sacred spaces. We must turn that passion into real action.

An amazing and scalable bonus of saving historical buildings is that the investment contributes to demand for the skills that make those buildings possible. Such excellent tradesmen in masonry, carpentry, glass and more are disappearing, but putting them to work on repairs builds demand and training for more. If you want even more good buildings, you cannot get them without the people.

Many people in tech have made, or are about to make, large sums of capital. I want to make a petition for more philanthropy towards historical buildings, specifically Churches. Every year, dozens of historical Churches fall into disrepair around Europe. Hundreds of these deconsecrated buildings are up for sale. You could save one for low six figures. A very wealthy person could save a lot of them.

I don't believe you need to be Christian to recognise the value in saving these buildings. You just have to respect history. These Churches should not become financial assets. Not yoga studios, not backdrops for light shows, not a rental opportunity. We should allow them to remain as sacred spaces. The preservation of our history is enough.

People will say, "Oh, but these are money drains! They will just need continuous repair, and you'll have nobody to sell them to!" Consider perhaps that in this you will find the whole point.
