title: Why save them
description: Why old Churches are worth saving, and what heritage custodianship is.
---

Churches have been in decline in communities across the West for decades. But you don't need to be Christian to recognise the value in saving these buildings. You just have to respect history.

For over a millennium, Churches have been the cradle of society. In their most desperate moments, people fled to Churches and prayed for better outcomes. In their most hopeful moments, Churches are where people came to give thanks. And the walls of even the supposedly lowliest Churches are still full of that energy: the human striving, the hopes and prayers, and the love and beauty. Every part of human life has been brought into these buildings.

If you go and stand in Churches intentionally and quieten your mind, I believe you can feel this energy, ever-present, like an atmospheric residue. Sometimes it suffocates and overwhelms. Sometimes it enlightens and inspires. There is always something in these buildings waiting for us to notice it. For these reasons, and regardless of our religious beliefs, we should come to these buildings with respect for all those who stood there before us, and recognise the value of preserving this history.

Many of us complain that beauty is receding from daily life, and that the buildings that once held civilisations together are sold to developers who see square footage and not the centuries. Sacred spaces like Churches are being turned into yoga studios, rental opportunities or backdrops for light shows. And while many of us complain about the death of these sacred spaces and of the crafts that built them, complaining is not enough. We must turn that passion into real action.

This action could be called heritage custodianship: taking on a historical building, repairing it where it needs it, and keeping it standing for the sole purpose of preserving its history and keeping a sacred space. It is also a beautiful way to learn about history, about the world, and to learn more about your own values. Heritage custodianship should perhaps be a greater force in philanthropy than it currently is.

Millions of us travel across the world to see ancient sites. Many of these buildings were religious, and they often survived because a new faith took them over and kept using them. The Parthenon was a temple, then a Church, then a mosque. The pyramids are still here only because their sheer mass meant nobody could take them apart. Every generation overlooks its most recent relics. But centuries from now our descendants will be glad that we preserved our more recent past, so that they too can visit and better understand these places and these times.

A side effect of such custodianship is that the investment creates demand for the skills that make buildings like these possible. Excellent tradesmen in masonry, carpentry, glass and more are disappearing, but employing them on projects like these builds demand and training for more. If we want more good buildings, we need these skills. What would it mean for our descendants to grow up in a world with no examples of things like stained glass windows, and no belief that such beautiful things are possible?

This website stemmed from a social media post encouraging more people to think about preserving Churches, after I took on a medieval Church restoration project myself. I have experienced the pushback that comes with taking on a building like this: that they are not good financial investments, that they need continuous repair, and that you will have nobody to sell them to. I contend that <em>in this exact experience of the immeasurable, you will find the whole point.</em>
