title: How repair costs are estimated
description: How the repair and upkeep figures on each church profile are worked out.
---

Every profile carries a rough cost of works and a yearly upkeep figure. They are estimates made from a desk. Treat them as a way to tell a 50,000 euro problem from a 500,000 euro one, and commission a survey before you offer.

1. **Measure from the listing.** Floor area and height give an approximate roof and wall area.
2. **Apply published rates.** In France in 2026 natural slate costs about 90 to 140 euros a square metre supplied and laid on an ordinary house, plus about 40 to strip the old roof. Lime repointing runs at 30 to 65 euros a square metre. A complete renovation of a historic house is quoted at 1,500 to 2,000 euros a square metre and up.
3. **Raise them for churches.** Scaffolding, height, heritage-approved materials and specialist trades roughly double house rates. Where there is no published rate, as with stained glass and painted decoration, the figure is an assumption and is marked as one.
4. **Check against real projects.** A typical church roof is reported at around 200,000 euros. The restoration of the small Saint-Roch chapel at Plounévez-Quintin in Brittany is estimated at over 200,000.
5. **Upkeep** covers insurance, property tax, gutter clearance, a yearly inspection and a reserve for the next roof.

**Scotland.** The Scottish churches are priced in pounds, with one real document as the yardstick. In December 2025 a quantity surveyor priced the repairs to St Andrew's at Tongue, a small Category A church on the north coast, from a conservation architect's survey. Reslating 215 square metres came to £53,750 for the slate, which is £250 a square metre, and about £90,500 once scaffolding, lead and the rest were added, which is about £420 a square metre. Repainting a harled elevation in mineral paint came to about £4,400. The other roofs are priced at £250 to £420 a square metre, and each line says so. Where a profile has no survey behind it the figures are assumptions. Source: [Tongue condition survey and costs, on the Highland community choices site](https://highland.communitychoices.scot/proposals/130-a-future-for-st-andrews-community-stewardship-of-an-historic-highland-landmark).
