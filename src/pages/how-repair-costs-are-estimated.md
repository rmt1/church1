title: How repair costs are estimated
description: How the repair and upkeep figures on each church profile are worked out.
---

Every profile carries a rough cost of works and a yearly upkeep figure. This is how they are estimated.

1. **We search for everything we can find.** The listing is the start. We then do a deep dive of the internet for more photographs of the church, heritage records, local history and press reports, so we know as much about the building as can be known without visiting it.
2. **We measure from the listing.** Floor area and height give a rough roof and wall area.
3. **We work out what the building needs, in three stages.** "Now" is the work that makes it safe and dry: the roof, the gutters, the drains, keeping people and water out. "Next" is the fabric of the building: the stonework, the plaster, the timber and the glass. "Later" is the interior and the services, which bring the church back into proper use.
4. **We apply published rates.** We look at published rates for materials and for labour in each country and apply them to our measurements. These are rough, but we hope to be directionally correct.
5. **We increase costs where a church needs it.** Scaffolding, height and heritage rules all add cost, and we increase the figures to allow for them. Stained glass and painted decoration have few published rates, so there we make assumptions from our own knowledge.
6. **We compare with real projects.** We check our totals against other documented restorations that we know of or have found online, and against our own knowledge.
7. **Upkeep** covers insurance, property tax, gutter clearance and a yearly inspection, plus a reserve for anything else that comes up.

These figures are meant to be directional only. Architect's fees may add 10 to 15%.
